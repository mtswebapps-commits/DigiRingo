A customer texts your business at 9:40 on a Tuesday night. They have a question, a problem, or money they want to spend. If nothing comes back, they don't know whether you saw it, whether the number still works, or whether they should try the next business on their list.

An after-hours reply fixes that. It's a short message that tells the customer you got their text, when you'll be back, and what to do if it can't wait. It doesn't solve their problem tonight, but it stops the silence from doing damage.

This guide gives you ready-to-use wording for evenings, weekends, holidays, vacations and urgent requests, plus the mistakes that make these messages backfire. Use them as true automatic replies if your phone system supports that, or as saved text you paste in by hand.

> **Key takeaway:** A good after-hours reply does three things in under 300 characters: confirms the message arrived, gives a specific time you'll respond, and offers a path for anything urgent. Everything else is optional.

## Why a reply beats silence

When people text a business, they usually expect a faster answer than they would from email. Silence after hours creates doubt. The customer can't tell the difference between "closed for the night" and "doesn't care."

A reply changes how that wait feels:

- **It confirms delivery.** The customer knows the number is real and monitored.
- **It sets a deadline you control.** "We'll reply by 10am" turns an open-ended wait into a known one.
- **It filters urgency.** People with a real emergency get directed somewhere useful instead of texting again and again.
- **It keeps the conversation in your thread.** When you answer in the morning, the context is right there.

The goal isn't to look like you're always open. It's to look organized.

## The anatomy of a good after-hours text

Before the examples, here are the parts that make a message work. Most good replies use four of these five.

| Part | Example wording | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgement | "Thanks for your message." | Confirms it arrived |
| Status | "We're closed for the evening." | Explains the silence |
| Return time | "We'll reply by 9am tomorrow." | Sets a clear expectation |
| Urgent path | "For emergencies, call 555-0100." | Protects customers who can't wait |
| Identity | "– Bright Path Plumbing" | Reminds them who they texted |

Keep it short. A standard SMS segment is 160 characters (fewer if you use emoji or special characters), and longer messages are split into multiple segments that are each billed. Two segments is fine; five is a wall of text nobody reads.

## Evening and weekend examples

These work for the most common case: you're closed for the night or the weekend and will be back on a normal schedule.

1. "Thanks for texting Bright Path Plumbing. We're closed for the evening and will reply by 9am tomorrow."
2. "Hi! You've reached Oak Street Dental after hours. We'll get back to you first thing in the morning. If this is a dental emergency, call our after-hours line at 555-0100."
3. "Got your message. Our team is off for the weekend and back Monday at 8am. We'll reply then."
4. "Thanks for reaching out. We answer texts Monday to Friday, 9 to 5. We'll be in touch the next business morning."
5. "We're closed right now but your message is saved. Expect a reply before noon tomorrow. – Lena, Studio Nine"
6. "Hi, thanks for your text! We're out until Monday. If you're booking, reply with your preferred day and time and we'll confirm when we're back."

Notice the last one. It asks for the information you'll need anyway, so the morning reply can be a confirmation instead of the start of a back-and-forth.

## Holiday and vacation examples

Holidays and vacations are longer gaps, so the return date matters even more. Use an actual date, not "after the holidays."

1. "Happy holidays from Rivera Landscaping! We're closed Dec 24 to Dec 26 and will reply on Dec 27."
2. "Thanks for your message. Our office is closed for Thanksgiving and reopens Monday, Nov 30. We'll respond then."
3. "Hi, I'm away until June 14 with limited access to messages. I'll reply when I'm back. For anything urgent about an active project, email support@example.com."
4. "We're closed today for the public holiday. Normal hours resume tomorrow at 9am."
5. "Our shop is closed for our annual break until Jan 3. Online orders still ship. For order questions, visit our website's help page."
6. "Thanks for thinking of us! The team is taking a short break and returns Aug 19. We'll answer every message in the order it arrived."

If you'll be away for more than a few days, consider pointing people to an alternative: a colleague, a website form, or a partner business. A week of "we'll reply when we're back" can feel like a closed door.

## Emergency and urgent-request wording

Some businesses can't leave urgent requests hanging: property managers, home services, healthcare, IT support. Your after-hours reply should give a clear next step without promising something you can't deliver.

1. "We're closed, but if you have a burst pipe or no heat, call our emergency line now: 555-0100. Otherwise we'll reply at 8am."
2. "Thanks for your message. For lockouts or safety issues, call 555-0199. For everything else, we'll get back to you tomorrow morning."
3. "If this is a medical emergency, call 911. For appointment questions, we'll reply when the office opens at 8am."
4. "Server down? Reply URGENT and our on-call tech will be notified. All other requests are answered next business day."

A word of caution on option 4: only offer an "URGENT" keyword if someone really is watching for it. Promising a response and then not delivering is worse than no promise at all.

Also, never let an auto-reply be the only safety net for something life-threatening. Point people to emergency services first.

## Setting up hours for after-hours replies

Whether the reply is automatic or manual, you need clear rules about when it applies. Write them down before you write any messages.

- **Define your hours.** Example: weekdays 8am to 6pm, Saturdays 9am to 1pm, closed Sundays.
- **Decide on time zone.** If your customers are spread across zones, state yours: "We'll reply by 9am Eastern."
- **Plan holiday dates in advance.** Put them in a calendar at the start of the year so the right message goes out on the right day.
- **Pick a response-time promise you can keep.** "By 10am" is better than "first thing" if your mornings are busy.
- **Limit repeats.** If a customer sends five texts in a row, they should get one auto-reply, not five.

If your phone system doesn't support automatic replies yet, the same rules still help. They tell whoever checks the phone in the morning what to send and in what order to work through the backlog.

## Mistakes to avoid

A badly worded reply can annoy customers more than silence. Watch for these:

- **Vague return times.** "We'll get back to you soon" means nothing at 11pm.
- **Fake urgency.** "Your message is important to us!" sounds like a phone queue. Skip it.
- **Marketing in the reply.** Someone asking for help doesn't want a 20% off coupon. Save promotions for people who opted in to them.
- **Wrong hours.** If you changed your schedule and forgot to update the message, you've just told customers something false.
- **Replying to STOP.** Opt-out messages should get the standard opt-out confirmation and nothing else. If you use automation, make sure it doesn't send an after-hours reply to someone who just unsubscribed.
- **Too long.** If it scrolls, cut it.
- **No name.** A reply from an unknown number with no business name looks like spam.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should every business use an after-hours reply?

Most businesses that get texts from customers benefit from one. If you rarely get messages outside working hours, a manual reply the next morning may be enough.

### How long should an after-hours text be?

Aim for one SMS segment, about 160 characters, and no more than two. Shorter messages are cheaper and far more likely to be read in full.

### Can I include a link in an after-hours reply?

Yes, but use your own full domain instead of a public link shortener. Carriers often filter messages with generic shortened links, which can stop your reply from being delivered.

### Do after-hours replies need to follow texting rules?

Yes. They're business messages, so they should come from a registered number, identify your business, and never be sent to someone who has opted out.

### Does DIGIRINGO send automatic replies?

Not yet. Auto-replies are coming soon. Today you can send replies by hand from the web or Android app, using saved wording like the examples in this post.

### What if a customer texts again after the auto-reply?

Don't send the same reply twice in one evening. One acknowledgement per after-hours window is enough; answer the whole thread when you're back.
