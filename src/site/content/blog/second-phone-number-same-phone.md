Carrying two phones is the classic way to separate work from personal life, and almost everyone who tries it gets tired of it within a month. Two chargers, two sets of notifications, and one phone always left at home on the day you need it.

The good news is that you can almost certainly get a second number on the phone already in your pocket. There are four main ways to do it, and each one has a different trade-off between cost, flexibility and how much your carrier is involved.

This guide compares all four, then walks through setup on iPhone and Android.

> **Key takeaway:** You can add a second number with dual SIM, an eSIM, a carrier add-on line, or a second-number app. For a business line, an app is usually the quickest and most flexible option because it works on any phone and computer, needs no new carrier plan, and can be set up in minutes.

## Four ways to add a second number

| Option | How it works | Cost model | Works on a computer | Setup time | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physical dual SIM | Second SIM card in a dual-SIM phone | Second mobile plan | No | Store visit or mail | People who want two full mobile plans |
| eSIM | Digital SIM downloaded to your phone | Second mobile plan | No | Same day | Travelers and people with eSIM phones |
| Carrier add-on line | Extra number on your existing plan | Monthly add-on fee | Sometimes, via carrier app | Varies | People who want one carrier bill |
| Second-number app | Number hosted in the cloud, used in an app | Number fee plus usage | Yes | Minutes | Business lines, freelancers, remote work |

Before choosing, ask yourself three questions. Do you need the number on a computer as well as a phone? Do you want it to work when you're abroad without roaming charges? Will you ever want to share it with someone else or move it to another device? If any answer is yes, an app is usually the better fit.

## Dual SIM and eSIM: pros and limits

Many modern phones support two lines at once, either with two physical SIM slots or with one physical SIM plus an eSIM, or even two eSIMs. Each line is a full mobile service with its own carrier plan.

**Pros:**

- Each number is a regular mobile number, with native calling and texting in the phone's own apps.
- It works without an internet connection, using the mobile network.
- Good for travelers who want a local data plan alongside their home number.

**Limits:**

- You pay for a second mobile plan, which often costs more than a business app.
- The number lives in one handset. It won't ring on your laptop.
- If you change phones, you move the eSIM or SIM along with it, which can be fiddly.
- Mobile plans are personal products. Handing the line to an employee later or showing a business name is awkward.
- Messaging apps on your phone may mix both numbers' texts together, depending on the phone.

Dual SIM works well if you want two complete mobile services. For a business line, it's often more than you need and less flexible than you want.

## Carrier add-on lines

Some mobile carriers sell a second number that rings on your existing phone without a second SIM. It's billed on your current plan and typically managed through the carrier's app.

**Pros:** one bill, one provider, and calls usually come through the phone's native dialer.

**Limits:** availability depends heavily on your carrier and country, features vary, and you're tied to that carrier. If you switch carriers, the add-on may not come with you. Most add-ons are also designed for personal use and offer few business features like texting registration or ringing several devices.

If your carrier offers one, check the monthly cost and whether it works abroad and on Wi-Fi before signing up.

## Second-number apps

A second-number app gives you a virtual phone number hosted by the app provider. You make and take calls over the internet inside the app, and the number can also ring on a web browser on your computer. If you're not sure how that works, our explainer on [what a virtual phone number is](/blog/what-is-a-virtual-phone-number) covers the basics.

**Pros:**

- Works on the phone you already have, with no new SIM or plan.
- The same number can ring on your phone and computer at the same time.
- You can often choose numbers in different cities, toll-free numbers, or numbers in other countries.
- Calls from abroad work over Wi-Fi without roaming charges.
- The number isn't tied to a handset, so changing phones is just a matter of signing in.

**Limits:**

- Calls and texts in the app need an internet connection, though many apps can forward unanswered calls to your regular mobile number.
- Some banks and online services refuse to send verification codes to internet-based numbers.
- Business texting in the US requires A2P 10DLC registration, which takes a few days.

For anyone adding a business line, these limits are usually small compared to the flexibility. If you're comparing specific apps, see our [comparison of second phone number apps](/blog/best-second-phone-number-apps).

## Setting it up on iPhone

If you choose dual SIM or eSIM on iPhone, you add the line in Settings under Cellular or Mobile Data, using a QR code or carrier activation. You can then label each line (for example "Personal" and "Business") and choose which line is the default for calls, messages and data.

If you choose an app, the steps depend on whether the app has an iOS version. DIGIRINGO doesn't have an iOS app yet, but iPhone users can use the full web app in Safari:

1. Open Safari and go to [digiringo.com/app](/app), then create your account.
2. Use Safari's share menu and choose "Add to Home Screen" so the web app opens like an app.
3. Top up your wallet and pick your business number.
4. Allow microphone access when Safari asks, so you can make calls.
5. Set your iPhone's regular mobile number as your forwarding number. Calls not answered in the app within about 30 seconds will forward to your iPhone, so you won't miss calls when the web app isn't open.

## Setting it up on Android

On Android, dual SIM and eSIM lines are added under the phone's network or SIM settings. Menus vary by manufacturer, but most let you name each SIM and pick a default for calls, texts and data.

For a second-number app, Android has the advantage of a dedicated DIGIRINGO app:

1. Install DIGIRINGO from Google Play and create an account.
2. Allow notifications and microphone access so incoming calls ring and you can talk.
3. Top up your wallet with $5, $10, $20 or $50 and choose a number.
4. Optional: also sign in on the web app on your computer. Incoming calls will ring both devices at the same time.
5. Add your mobile number as a forwarding phone for calls you don't pick up in the app.

If your phone uses aggressive battery saving, exclude the app from battery optimization so notifications arrive reliably.
