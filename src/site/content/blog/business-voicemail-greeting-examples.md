Your voicemail greeting is often the first thing a new customer hears from your business. If it's muffled, rushed, or still the default robot voice reading out digits, callers notice. Many of them won't leave a message at all.

A good greeting is short, tells callers they reached the right place, and gives them a reason to leave a message. That's it. You don't need a script writer or a studio, just a quiet room and a few minutes.

Below are 25 greetings grouped by situation. Pick one, change the names and details, and record it. Then read the recording tips at the end, because how you say it matters almost as much as what you say.

> **Key takeaway:** Every business greeting needs four things: your business name, an apology-free "we can't take your call right now," what the caller should leave, and when they'll hear back. Keep it under 20 seconds.

## What every good greeting includes

Before the examples, here's the checklist they're built on.

| Element | Why it matters | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Confirms the caller dialed correctly | "You've reached Greenleaf Landscaping." |
| Status | Tells them nobody can pick up | "We're with other customers right now." |
| What to leave | Gets you useful messages | "Leave your name, number and what you need." |
| Callback promise | Sets expectations | "We'll call you back within one business day." |
| Optional alternative | Helps urgent callers | "For faster help, text this number." |

A few things to leave out:

- Long apologies. One "sorry we missed you" is plenty.
- Your full website address spelled out letter by letter.
- Promotions. Callers want to leave a message, not hear an ad.
- Saying the date, unless you'll update the greeting every day.

## General business greetings

1. "Hi, you've reached Greenleaf Landscaping. We can't come to the phone right now, but leave your name, number and a short message, and we'll call you back today."
2. "Thanks for calling Mason and Co. We're helping other customers at the moment. Please leave your name and number after the tone, and we'll get back to you within one business day."
3. "You've reached the office of Dr. Lina Ortiz. Please leave your name, phone number and the reason for your call, and our team will return your call as soon as possible."
4. "Hi, this is Sam at Northside Bike Repair. I'm probably elbow-deep in a bike right now. Leave a message and I'll call you back before the end of the day."

6. "You've reached Bloom Floral Studio. We're away from the phone, but we'd love to hear from you. Leave your name, number and the date of your event, and we'll be in touch shortly."
7. "Hello, you've reached Atlas Moving. If you're calling for a quote, please leave your name, number, moving date and the size of your home, and we'll call you back with a price."

## Recording tips for a clear, friendly sound

The words above are only half the job. A great script recorded badly still sounds unprofessional.

1. **Find a quiet, soft room.** Closets and bedrooms work better than kitchens or offices with hard walls. Turn off fans and air conditioning.
2. **Hold the phone or mic at a steady distance.** About a hand's width from your mouth is a good start.
3. **Smile while you talk.** It sounds silly, but it changes your tone and listeners can hear it.
4. **Slow down.** Most people rush their greeting. Read it about 20% slower than feels natural.
5. **Say numbers and names clearly.** If you give a callback time or website, pause before and after.
6. **Record a few takes.** Listen on a phone speaker, not headphones, and keep the best one.
7. **Update it when things change.** An old holiday greeting in March is worse than no greeting at all.

| Problem | Likely cause | Quick fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sounds echoey | Hard walls, empty room | Record near curtains, cushions or clothes |
| Too quiet | Phone held too far away | Move closer and speak up slightly |
| Pops on "p" and "b" | Breathing straight into mic | Angle the phone slightly to the side |
| Sounds bored | Reading flat from a script | Stand up, smile, and record again |
| Too long | Too many details | Cut everything except the four essentials |

## Choosing the right greeting for each line

If you have more than one number, give each its own greeting. A sales line should sound eager and promise a fast callback. A support line should ask for the details you need to solve the problem. An after-hours greeting should state your hours and offer an emergency route if you have one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a business voicemail greeting be?

Aim for 10 to 20 seconds. That's enough for your business name, a short status, what to leave, and when you'll call back. Longer greetings make callers hang up before the tone.

### Should I use my own voice or a recorded professional voice?

Your own voice is fine for most small businesses and often feels more personal. Record in a quiet room, speak slowly and smile. A professional voice can sound more polished but needs paying for again every time you change the wording.

### Should I mention my business hours in the greeting?

Yes for after-hours greetings, since it tells callers when to expect you. For daytime greetings it's optional, and leaving it out keeps the greeting shorter.

### How often should I update my voicemail greeting?

Update it whenever your hours, staff or services change, and before and after every holiday closure. Set a calendar reminder so a holiday greeting doesn't stay up for months.
