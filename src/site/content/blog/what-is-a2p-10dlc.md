If you've tried to send business texts from a regular US phone number recently, you may have hit a wall: messages that never arrive, or a provider telling you to "register for 10DLC" first. It sounds like bureaucracy, and partly it is. But it exists for a reason, and once you understand it, registration is a one-time task that takes an afternoon of paperwork and a few days of waiting.

A2P 10DLC is the system US mobile carriers use to identify businesses that send text messages from ordinary 10-digit phone numbers. Carriers want to know who is sending, what kind of messages they send, and how recipients agreed to receive them. Registered senders get better delivery. Unregistered senders increasingly get blocked.

This guide explains the terms in plain English, why the system exists, what you register, what it costs, and how it compares to toll-free numbers and short codes.

> **Key takeaway:** If your business sends texts to US customers from a local 10-digit number through any app or software, you need to register a brand and a campaign for A2P 10DLC. Unregistered traffic is heavily filtered or blocked by US carriers.

## What A2P and 10DLC mean

The name is two acronyms stuck together.

- **A2P (application-to-person)** means messages sent through software rather than typed on a personal phone to a friend. If your texts go through a business phone app, a CRM, a booking tool or an API, they're A2P, even if a human types each one.
- **P2P (person-to-person)** is ordinary texting between individuals on their personal phones.
- **10DLC (10-digit long code)** is a standard 10-digit phone number, like (512) 555-0134, as opposed to a 5- or 6-digit short code or a toll-free number.

So A2P 10DLC simply means "business texting from regular local phone numbers." The registration system around it is what most people mean when they say "10DLC."

## Why carriers made registration mandatory

For years, local numbers were treated as person-to-person lines. Businesses and spammers alike sent large volumes through them with little oversight. Carriers responded by filtering heavily, which also caught legitimate businesses.

10DLC registration was the industry's fix. The major US carriers worked with an industry registry so that every business sender is identified and every type of messaging is described up front. In exchange, registered businesses get a clearer path to reliable delivery.

The goals are straightforward:

- Know who is sending (a verified business identity).
- Know what they're sending (a described use case with sample messages).
- Know that recipients agreed (a documented opt-in process).
- Make it easy to trace and stop abuse.

Over the past few years, carriers moved from warning unregistered senders to blocking most unregistered 10DLC traffic. Today, registration is effectively required for any business texting from local numbers.

## Brands, campaigns and the registry

Registration has two main parts, both handled through The Campaign Registry (TCR), an industry database the carriers use. You normally don't sign up with TCR yourself; your texting provider submits the information for you.

| Part | What it describes | What you provide |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | Who you are | Legal business name, tax ID (EIN in the US), address, website, contact details, business type |
| Campaign | What you send and how people opt in | Use case, description, sample messages, opt-in method, opt-out and help wording |
| Number assignment | Which numbers send the campaign | The phone numbers linked to the approved campaign |

A **brand** is your business identity. It's checked against public records, which is why your legal name and tax ID must match exactly. Very small businesses without an EIN can often register as a sole proprietor, which has lower limits.

A **campaign** is a specific messaging program, like appointment reminders, customer care or marketing. Each campaign has a use case category, which affects how it's reviewed and what volume limits apply. ## Fees and timelines

Fees are set by the registry and carriers, and providers usually pass them through. The exact amounts change from time to time, so treat these as the types of costs to expect rather than fixed numbers.

| Fee type | When it's charged | Rough size |
|---|---|---|
| Brand registration | Once per brand | A few dollars |
| Brand vetting (if required) | Once, for higher limits | Tens of dollars |
| Campaign vetting | Once per campaign submission | Small one-time fee |
| Monthly campaign fee | Every month the campaign is active | A few dollars up to around $10, depending on use case |
| Carrier pass-through fees | Per message, on some carriers | Fractions of a cent |

Timelines vary too. Brand checks can be fast. Campaign review typically takes anywhere from one to several business days, and longer if the campaign is sent back for corrections. Plan for about a week from start to sending, and build in extra time if you're launching something time-sensitive.

## What happens if you don't register

Sending business texts from an unregistered local number causes problems that get worse over time.

- **Messages are blocked or silently filtered.** Recipients may never see them, and you may not get a clear error.
- **Your number's reputation suffers.** Repeated blocked traffic can make future delivery harder.
- **Your provider may restrict you.** Most providers won't let unregistered numbers send to US mobiles at all.
- **Higher legal risk.** Registration doesn't make you compliant with consent laws, but the opt-in documentation it requires pushes you toward good practice.

Registration is about deliverability. Consent is a separate legal duty. Our [SMS opt-in and consent guide](/blog/sms-opt-in-consent-guide) covers what you need to collect and keep.

## 10DLC vs toll-free vs short code

10DLC isn't the only way to text customers in the US. Here's how the three main options compare.

| | 10DLC (local number) | Toll-free number | Short code |
|---|---|---|---|
| Looks like | (512) 555-0134 | (888) 555-0134 | 5 or 6 digits, like 55512 |
| Approval process | Brand + campaign registration | Toll-free verification | Carrier approval per program |
| Typical setup time | Days | Days to weeks | Weeks to months |
| Cost | Low | Low to moderate | High (usually thousands per year) |
| Voice calls too | Yes | Yes | No |
| Best for | Local businesses, conversations, reminders | National brands, support lines | High-volume alerts and marketing |

Most small businesses choose 10DLC because customers recognize a local number, it supports both calls and texts, and it's the cheapest to run.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need 10DLC if I only send a few texts a day?

Yes, if you send business texts from a local number through an app or software. Volume doesn't exempt you. Low-volume senders can usually choose a lighter registration option with lower monthly fees.

### Does 10DLC apply to texting Canadian numbers?

10DLC is a US carrier system. Canadian carriers have their own rules, and requirements can differ by provider, so check with your texting provider for messages to Canadian recipients.

### How long does 10DLC approval take?

Brand checks are often quick, while campaign review usually takes from one to several business days. On DIGIRINGO, carrier approval typically takes 1 to 7 days.

### Does registration make my texts legally compliant?

No. Registration identifies you to carriers and improves delivery. You still need proper consent, clear opt-out handling and sensible sending practices under laws like the TCPA.

### Can I use one registration for several phone numbers?

Generally yes. One brand can have campaigns, and several numbers can be assigned to an approved campaign, as long as they're used for the messaging described in it.
