If your business has suppliers in one country, clients in another and a contractor somewhere else, international calls become a regular cost. Placed the wrong way, a single long call can cost more than a month of domestic calling. Placed the right way, the same call can cost a few cents a minute or less.

The difference comes down to how the call travels and who you pay for it. Mobile carriers, calling cards, free chat apps and VoIP providers all handle international calls differently, and each has trade-offs in price, convenience and how professional you look to the person you're calling.

This guide compares the main options, explains how international per-minute pricing works, and shares tips for clearer long-distance calls and showing a local number to customers abroad.

> **Key takeaway:** For regular international business calls, VoIP calling over the internet is usually the cheapest option that still reaches ordinary phone numbers. Check the per-minute rate for the specific country and whether you're calling a mobile or a landline, because those two factors drive the price.

## Why carrier international rates are so high

Standard mobile and landline plans are built around domestic calling. International calls are often an add-on, and without a specific international pack they can be charged at high pay-per-minute rates.

A few reasons this happens:

- **Termination fees.** When your call reaches another country, the local network charges to complete it. Carriers pass that on, often with a large margin.
- **Bundled plans.** Domestic minutes are "unlimited," so carriers recover costs elsewhere, including international calls.
- **Roaming confusion.** Calling internationally from home and calling while travelling abroad (roaming) are priced differently, and both can be expensive.
- **Little price pressure.** Most customers rarely call abroad, so they don't compare international rates.

If your carrier offers an international add-on, it may be worth it for frequent calls to one country. For calls to many countries, or occasional calls, other options are often cheaper.

| Free chat apps (app-to-app) | No, only other app users | Free over data | Not a business number | Contacts who use the same app |
| VoIP business phone | Yes | Low per minute, varies by country | Shows your business number | Regular business calls |

Free app-to-app calling is genuinely free, but only works when the other person uses the same app and is willing to take a business call there. Many suppliers, banks and government offices still need a normal phone call.

## VoIP per-minute rates explained

VoIP providers carry your call over the internet and hand it to a phone network close to the destination. That keeps the expensive part of the route short.

You'll usually pay a per-minute rate that depends on:

- **The destination country.** Some countries are cheap to reach; others have high local termination costs.
- **The type of number you're calling.** Mobiles and landlines often have different rates in the same country.
- **Special number ranges.** Premium, satellite or certain special-service numbers can cost much more.
- **Billing increments.** Some providers bill per second, others round up to the minute.

Always check the rate for the exact country and number type before a long call. A good provider shows this clearly before you dial.

## Mobile vs landline destination pricing

In many countries, calling a mobile costs more than calling a landline, sometimes several times more. That's because mobile networks charge higher fees to complete incoming calls.

Practical tips:

- **Ask for a landline number** for regular calls with suppliers or offices, if they have one.
- **Check which type a number is** if the rate list separates them. Some countries make this easy to tell from the number's prefix.
- **Keep long calls on the cheaper type** and use short calls or messages for mobiles.
- **Consider texting** for quick updates, but check international SMS rates too; they also vary by country.

## Call quality tips for long-distance calls

Internet calls to the other side of the world can sound as clear as local ones. When they don't, it's usually the connection at your end. A few simple habits help:

- **Use a stable connection.** Wired internet or strong Wi-Fi beats a weak mobile signal.
- **Close bandwidth-heavy apps** like video streaming or large uploads during the call.
- **Use a headset** to cut echo and background noise.
- **Pause briefly** before responding. A small delay is normal on very long routes, and pausing avoids talking over each other.
- **Call at sensible local times,** which is good manners and means the other person picks up somewhere quiet.

## Showing a local caller ID abroad

Cost isn't the only thing that matters. Customers abroad are more likely to answer a local-looking number than an unknown foreign one, and more likely to call you back if it doesn't cost them an international call.

The approach is simple:

1. **Get a virtual number in the country** where your customers are.
2. **Call from that number** so it shows as the caller ID.
3. **Let customers call that number** and have it ring your app wherever you are.

Some countries require ID or address documents before a number can be issued. Our guide to [international virtual numbers](/blog/international-virtual-numbers) explains what to expect.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest way to make international business calls?

For calls to regular phone numbers, VoIP calling is usually among the cheapest options. Free app-to-app calls cost nothing but only reach people using the same app.

### Why does calling a mobile abroad cost more than a landline?

Mobile networks in many countries charge higher fees to complete incoming calls, and providers pass that cost on. Rate lists often show separate prices for each.

### Can I make international calls from my computer?

Yes. With a browser-based phone app like DIGIRINGO, you can call international numbers directly from your computer with a headset.

### Do international calls over the internet sound worse?

Not usually. With a stable connection and a headset, they can sound very similar to local calls. Very long routes may add a slight delay.

### Can customers abroad call me without paying international rates?

Yes, if you get a local number in their country. They call a local number, and the call rings your app wherever you are.

### How do I know what a call will cost before I make it?

Check the per-minute rate for the destination and number type, then multiply by the expected length. DIGIRINGO shows per-country rates in the app's rate list.
