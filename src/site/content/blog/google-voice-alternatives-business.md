Google Voice is often the first second number people try. It's familiar, it works in the browser and on mobile, and the personal version costs nothing for a US number. For a freelancer taking a handful of calls, that can be plenty.

As a business grows, though, the limits start to show: texting rules, team features, international reach and support. None of this makes Google Voice a bad product. It just means it was designed with certain users in mind, and a growing business may not be one of them.

This guide covers what Google Voice does well, where it tends to fall short for businesses, what to look for in an alternative, and how several popular options compare. We describe other products from general knowledge as of late 2026; features and prices change, so always check each provider's current pages before you decide.

> **Key takeaway:** Google Voice is a solid, low-cost option for individuals in the US. If you need registered business texting, numbers outside the US, flexible call routing or pay-as-you-go pricing without per-user plans, it's worth comparing alternatives.

## What Google Voice does well

Credit where it's due. Google Voice gets several things right:

- **Easy to start.** If you have a Google account, setup takes minutes.
- **Free personal tier** for a US number with calls and texts within the US, which is hard to beat for casual use.
- **Works across devices,** including browser, Android and iPhone.
- **Voicemail transcription** that many people find useful.
- **Call forwarding** to linked phones.
- **A business version** that's part of Google Workspace, with paid tiers aimed at teams.

If you're a solo professional who mainly needs a separate US number and already lives in Google's tools, it's a reasonable choice.

## Where it falls short for businesses

The issues businesses most often run into, broadly speaking:

- **The personal version isn't designed for business use.** Google's terms for the free tier are aimed at personal accounts, and features for compliant business texting are limited.
- **Business texting rules.** US carriers expect business texts to come from registered numbers (A2P 10DLC). If your provider doesn't give you a clear route to register, your messages may be filtered as volume grows.
- **US-centric numbers.** Google Voice numbers are primarily US numbers. If you need a local number in the UK, Australia or elsewhere, you'll need another provider.
- **Tied to Google Workspace for business features.** The business version requires a Workspace subscription and is priced per user, which can add up for small teams that only need one shared line.
- **Limited routing options.** If you want to send calls to a SIP device, an AI agent or your own system, options are narrower than with providers built around routing.
- **Support.** Free personal accounts rely mostly on help articles and community forums.
- **Inactivity rules.** Personal numbers that aren't used for a long time can be reclaimed, so check the current policy if you rely on the number.

## Features to look for in an alternative

Before you compare products, decide what actually matters to you. Here's a checklist:

- **Business texting with registration support,** ideally a guided 10DLC process inside the app.
- **Number choice:** local, toll-free and international numbers.
- **Device coverage:** browser, Android, iPhone, and whether incoming calls ring all devices at once.
- **Call forwarding and routing:** forward to a cellphone, send to SIP devices, or hand off to your own systems.
- **Voicemail** and how you access it.
- **Pricing model:** per-user subscription vs pay-as-you-go. Per-user plans suit larger teams; pay-as-you-go suits low or uneven usage.
- **International calling rates** if you call abroad.
- **Team features** like shared numbers, seats and roles, if several people answer.
- **Porting** if you need to bring your existing number.
- **Support** you can reach when something breaks.

## Moving your number off Google Voice

If customers know your Google Voice number, you'll probably want to keep it. The general process is:

1. **Check that your new provider accepts ports** from Google Voice. Not all do, and some only accept certain number types.
2. **Unlock the number** in your Google Voice settings. Google has a process for releasing a number for transfer; check its current help page for the steps and any fee.
3. **Submit the port request** with your new provider, using the account details they ask for.
4. **Keep the Google Voice account active** until the port completes.
5. **Test calls and texts** on the new provider once it's done.

If you'd rather not port, you can run both numbers side by side for a while: get a new business number, tell customers about it, and keep the old one forwarding until calls dwindle. Our guide on [what number porting is](/blog/what-is-number-porting) covers the full lifecycle.

## Which option fits which business

A rough guide:

- **Solo professional in the US, light usage, already in Google's ecosystem:** Google Voice may be all you need.
- **Small team that wants a shared inbox and many collaboration features:** look at per-user platforms focused on teams.
- **Business that needs desk phones and a traditional setup:** providers with hardware options.
- **Business with low or uneven call volume, international customers, or a need for flexible routing:** a pay-as-you-go provider without seat fees, such as DIGIRINGO.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use Google Voice for my business?

Many sole traders do, but the free personal version is designed for personal use. Google offers a business version through Google Workspace, priced per user.

### What is the best Google Voice alternative?

It depends on your needs. Team-focused platforms suit larger groups; pay-as-you-go providers suit low or international usage. Make a list of must-have features and compare against it.

### Can I text customers from Google Voice?

You can send texts, but for business texting in the US you'll want a provider that supports A2P 10DLC registration, so carriers don't filter your messages as volume grows.

### Can I move my Google Voice number to DIGIRINGO?

Not yet. Number porting into DIGIRINGO is coming soon. In the meantime, you can get a new DIGIRINGO number and forward your Google Voice number to it while customers switch over.

### Is there a free alternative to Google Voice for business?

Few business-grade services are completely free, because numbers and calls cost providers money. Pay-as-you-go services can keep costs to a few dollars a month for light use.
