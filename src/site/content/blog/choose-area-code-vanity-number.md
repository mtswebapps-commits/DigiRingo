Your phone number gets seen before anyone hears your voice. It shows up on a caller's screen, on your website, on the side of a van and at the bottom of every email. The first three digits tell people where you are, or at least where you want them to think you are. The rest decides whether anyone can remember it.

Most people grab whichever number comes up first when they sign up for a phone service. That's often fine. But a few minutes spent choosing the area code and looking for a cleaner number can pay off for years, because changing a business number later is a real hassle.

This guide covers why area codes still matter, how to search for a number you like, when a vanity or repeating-digit number is worth paying extra for, and what to do if the number you want belongs to someone else.

> **Key takeaway:** Pick an area code that matches where your customers are, not where you happen to live. Then look for a number that's easy to say out loud. Vanity numbers help in some trades, but a clean local number is enough for most small businesses.

## Why the area code still matters to customers

Mobile phones and number portability mean an area code no longer proves where someone is. Plenty of people keep a number from a city they left ten years ago. Even so, callers still read area codes as a signal.

- **Local trust.** People are more likely to pick up a call from a number that looks local. An unknown out-of-state number is easy to ignore as a possible sales call.
- **"We're nearby."** For service businesses like plumbers, cleaners, tutors and contractors, a local area code tells customers you actually work in their area.
- **Cost perception.** Older customers in particular may still assume calling a distant area code costs more, even though most plans no longer charge for it.
- **Market presence.** If you sell into a city you're not based in, a number with that city's area code helps you look established there.

If your customers are spread across the whole country, a single local area code matters less, and a toll-free number may suit you better. We compare the two in [local vs toll-free numbers](/blog/local-vs-toll-free-number).

## Searching numbers by city, prefix or digits

Most business phone providers let you look through the numbers they have available before you commit. The usual ways to search are:

| Search method | What it means | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| By city or region | Shows numbers tied to a city's rate centre | Local service businesses |
| By area code | Shows numbers starting with a chosen three-digit code | Targeting a specific market |
| By prefix | Matches the first six digits (area code plus exchange) | Matching an existing office line |
| By digits or pattern | Finds numbers containing certain digits | Memorable or vanity numbers |

A few practical points:

- **Inventory changes constantly.** Numbers get claimed and released every day. If you see one you like, don't wait a week to think about it.
- **Some area codes are tight.** Busy metro codes can have limited inventory. Overlay codes (a newer code covering the same area as an older one) often have more numbers available and are just as local.
- **Try a few nearby cities.** If your exact city has nothing good, a neighbouring city in the same area code usually works just as well for customers.

## Vanity and repeating-digit numbers

A vanity number spells a word on the keypad, like a plumber's number ending in "PIPE" or a florist with "ROSE." A repeating-digit or patterned number is easy to remember for a different reason: 555-2000, 444-1234 and so on.

When vanity numbers make sense:

- **Advertising you can't click.** Radio, billboards, vehicle wraps and TV ads give people a few seconds to remember a number. A word sticks better than digits.
- **One-word businesses.** If your business can be summed up in a short word, a vanity number can double as a brand.
- **Toll-free lines.** Vanity toll-free numbers are the classic example, because they aren't tied to any region.

When they're not worth it:

- **Most customers find you online.** If people click a "call" button on your website or Google profile, nobody types the number by hand.
- **The word is a stretch.** A vanity number that needs explaining ("it's 1-800-GET-FIXD, no E") does more harm than good.
- **The premium is high.** Highly sought-after numbers can be sold at a premium by some providers or brokers. A clean ordinary number may give you 90 percent of the benefit for a fraction of the cost.

A middle ground is a number that's simply easy to say: few distinct digits, a round ending like 00 or 50, or a pattern such as 1212. These are easier to find than a perfect word and much easier for callers to repeat.

## Can you keep a number you find elsewhere?

Sometimes the number you want already exists. Maybe it's your old landline, a mobile number your customers already know, or a number listed on a provider you're leaving.

If the number is yours, you can usually move it to a new provider through **number porting**. Porting is a regulated process that transfers the number from one carrier to another without changing it. You'll typically need the account holder's name, the service address, the account number and a port-out PIN from your current carrier. We explain the full process in [number porting explained](/blog/what-is-number-porting).

If the number belongs to someone else, there's usually no clean way to get it. Some numbers are resold by brokers, especially toll-free vanity numbers, but you should be careful. Make sure the transfer happens through a proper port with the right paperwork, never just a promise.

## Mistakes to avoid

A few common errors cost businesses more than they expect:

1. **Choosing your home area code by default.** If you live in one city but your customers are in another, use theirs.
2. **Picking a number with confusing digits.** Long runs of similar digits like 6-8-8-6-8 are easy to mishear over the phone.
3. **Paying a big premium for a vanity number nobody will type.** Check how customers actually reach you first.
4. **Printing the number before it's working.** Test calls and texts in and out before ordering signs, cards or ads.
5. **Using a number with a messy history.** Recycled numbers sometimes still get calls meant for the previous owner. If you start getting lots of unrelated calls in the first weeks, it may be worth swapping early while it's cheap to do so.
6. **Forgetting about texting.** If you plan to text customers in the US, your number will need 10DLC registration. That's fine for any local number, but plan for it, since carrier approval can take several days.

## Local, toll-free or international: quick guide

| Your situation | Suggested number |
|---|---|
| Service business in one city | Local number in that city's area code |
| Selling into a second city | A second local number for that market |
| Customers across the whole country | Toll-free, or a local number plus toll-free |
| Customers in another country | A local number in that country |
| Heavy radio or billboard advertising | Consider a vanity or easy-pattern number |

Having more than one number is normal now. Many small businesses keep a local number for their home market and add another for a new city or country, with all of them ringing the same app.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the area code affect how much calls cost?

For callers in the US and Canada, almost never. Nearly all phone plans include nationwide calling, so a customer pays the same to call any local area code.

### Can I have numbers in several area codes?

Yes. Many businesses keep one number per market. With DIGIRINGO you can hold several numbers on one account and choose which one to call out from.

### Is a vanity number worth it for a small business?

Only if people need to remember your number without clicking it, such as from radio, billboards or vehicles. If most customers find you online, a clean local number works just as well.

### What's an overlay area code?

It's a second area code added to the same region when the first one runs low on numbers. Numbers in an overlay code are just as local as the original.
