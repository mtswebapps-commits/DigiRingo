Most 10DLC rejections aren't about the business. They're about the paperwork: a legal name that doesn't match the tax record, a website with no privacy policy, a campaign description that says "we send texts to customers," or sample messages that don't look like anything a real business would send.

The good news is that these problems are easy to avoid if you prepare before you start. Registration itself is mostly filling in forms. What makes the difference is giving reviewers a clear, consistent picture of who you are, what you'll send, and how people agree to receive it.

This walkthrough covers what to gather, how to register your brand and campaign, examples of descriptions and sample messages that read well to reviewers, and the most common reasons for rejection. If you're new to the topic, start with our explainer on [what A2P 10DLC is](/blog/what-is-a2p-10dlc).

> **Key takeaway:** Approval comes down to consistency. Your legal name, tax ID, website, campaign description, sample messages and opt-in process should all describe the same business doing the same thing. Prepare everything before you open the form.

## Before you start: what information you need

Gather these details first. Having them ready saves you from submitting half-finished forms that get rejected.

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Legal business name | Exactly as it appears on your tax registration, including "LLC" or "Inc." |
| Tax ID | US EIN for most businesses; sole proprietors may register differently |
| Business type | For example private company, public company, nonprofit, sole proprietor |
| Business address | Must match your registration records; avoid PO boxes where possible |
| Website | Live, working, and clearly about your business |
| Privacy policy | Published on your website, mentioning how you handle phone numbers |
| Contact person | Name, email and phone of someone responsible for the account |
| Messaging plan | What you'll send, to whom, and how they opt in |

Check your website before you submit. Reviewers often visit it. It should show your business name, what you do, contact details, and a privacy policy. If you collect phone numbers on a web form, that form should include the consent wording you describe in your campaign.

## Registering your brand

The brand is your business identity. Your provider sends it to The Campaign Registry, where it's checked against public data.

1. **Enter your legal name exactly.** "Smith Plumbing LLC" and "Smith Plumbing" are different to a matching system.
2. **Enter your EIN.** Double-check every digit. A wrong digit is one of the most common causes of failure.
3. **Choose the right entity type.** Picking "public company" when you're a small private LLC causes a mismatch.
4. **Add your address and website.** Use the address on your tax records.
5. **Add a contact.** Use a real email that someone checks; reviewers or your provider may need to reach you.
6. **Submit and wait for the brand check.** This is often quick. If it fails, the issue is almost always a data mismatch.

Sole proprietors without an EIN usually have a separate, simpler registration path. It typically comes with lower sending limits and fewer campaigns, which is fine for many one-person businesses.

## Choosing the right campaign use case

A campaign describes one messaging program. The use case you choose tells reviewers what to expect. Pick the one that honestly matches what you'll send.

| Use case | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Customer care | Replying to customer questions, support conversations | Don't include promotions |
| Account notifications | Order updates, account alerts | Must relate to an existing account |
| Delivery notifications | Shipping and arrival updates | Keep it about deliveries |
| Marketing | Offers, promotions, announcements | Needs clear marketing consent |
| Mixed | A combination of the above | Must describe each type; reviewed closely |
| Low volume mixed | Small senders with several message types | Lower throughput limits |
| Two-factor authentication | One-time codes and login verification | Codes only, no other content |

Many small businesses choose customer care or a mixed or low-volume mixed campaign, because they reply to customers and send the occasional reminder. If you plan to send promotions, say so. Sending marketing on a campaign registered as customer care is a fast way to get your campaign suspended.

## Writing the campaign description, sample messages and opt-in

This is where most approvals are won or lost. Be specific and concrete.

### Campaign description

Weak: "We send texts to our customers."

Strong: "Greenleaf Landscaping sends appointment confirmations, reminders and on-the-way notices to residential customers who book lawn and garden services. Customers also text us questions, and our staff reply manually. Messages are sent only to customers who provided their mobile number when booking and agreed to receive texts."

### Sample messages

Write two to five messages that look exactly like what you'll send. Include your business name and, in at least one, opt-out wording.

- "Greenleaf Landscaping: your lawn service is booked for Tue, Oct 14 between 9-11am. Reply C to confirm or call us to reschedule. Reply STOP to opt out."
- "Hi Dana, it's Greenleaf Landscaping. Our crew is on the way and should arrive in about 20 minutes."
- "Greenleaf Landscaping: thanks for your question. Yes, we can add hedge trimming to Thursday's visit. It's $45. Reply YES to add it."

### Opt-in description

Describe exactly how a person agrees to receive texts, where they see the wording, and what it says.

"Customers opt in when booking on our website or by phone. The booking form has an unchecked checkbox reading: 'I agree to receive appointment texts from Greenleaf Landscaping. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help.' Phone bookings are confirmed verbally and recorded in our booking system."

For more wording examples, see our [SMS opt-in and consent guide](/blog/sms-opt-in-consent-guide).

### Opt-out and help messages

Have ready replies for STOP and HELP. For example: "You've been unsubscribed from Greenleaf Landscaping texts. No more messages will be sent." and "Greenleaf Landscaping: for help call (512) 555-0134 or visit our website. Reply STOP to opt out."

## Common rejection reasons

| Rejection reason | What it usually means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Brand mismatch | Name or EIN doesn't match records | Copy the name exactly from your tax documents |
| Website not working or unrelated | Reviewers can't verify the business | Publish a simple site with your name, services and contact |
| No privacy policy | Missing or doesn't cover phone data | Add a policy stating you don't sell or share mobile numbers |
| Vague description | Reviewers can't tell what you'll send | Name the business, message types and audience |
| Samples don't match use case | Promotions in a customer care campaign | Choose the right use case or remove promo samples |
| Unclear opt-in | No explanation of how people consent | Describe the form, checkbox or keyword and its wording |
| Missing opt-out language | No STOP instructions | Add "Reply STOP to opt out" to samples and opt-in text |
| Prohibited content | Topics carriers restrict | Check provider rules before you apply |

If a campaign is rejected, read the reason carefully, fix exactly that issue, and make sure the change is consistent everywhere (description, samples and website). Resubmitting without changes usually gets the same result.

## After approval: limits and best practices

Approval isn't the end. Carriers monitor traffic, and campaigns can be suspended if what you send doesn't match what you registered.

- **Stay within your use case.** If your messaging changes, update or add a campaign.
- **Respect throughput limits.** Lower-tier registrations have daily and per-minute limits; spread out bulk sends.
- **Honor opt-outs immediately.** Never text a number that replied STOP.
- **Identify yourself.** Start messages with your business name, especially the first one.
- **Avoid public link shorteners.** Use your own domain for links where possible.
- **Watch delivery receipts.** A sudden drop in delivery can signal filtering. See [why business texts aren't delivered](/blog/business-texts-not-delivered).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I register for 10DLC without a website?

It's much harder. Reviewers use your website to confirm your business exists and to check your privacy policy and opt-in process. A simple one-page site with your name, services, contact details and privacy policy is usually enough.

### Can a sole proprietor register for 10DLC?

Yes. Sole proprietors without an EIN can usually register through a dedicated sole proprietor path. It typically allows one campaign and lower sending volumes.

### What should I do if my campaign is rejected?

Read the rejection reason, fix that specific issue, and check that your description, sample messages and website all tell the same story. Then resubmit. Each resubmission may involve a small vetting fee, so it pays to get it right.

### Can I change my campaign after it's approved?

Some details can be updated, but major changes to your use case usually mean registering a new campaign. If you start sending a new type of message, register it first.

### How many campaigns do I need?

Most small businesses need one. If you send very different programs, such as support conversations and a separate marketing list, a second campaign keeps each one clearly described.
