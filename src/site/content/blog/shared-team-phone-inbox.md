In a lot of small businesses, one person "owns" the phone. Customers call or text that person, and everything works fine until they're on a job site, on holiday, or simply busy. Then messages pile up, callers wait, and nobody else can see what's going on.

A shared team inbox fixes this by putting calls and texts for one business number where several people can see and answer them. Done well, customers get faster replies and your team stops stepping on each other. Done badly, two people answer the same customer while a third message sits untouched.

This guide explains how shared business numbers work, the habits that make them work, and how to roll one out to a small team without chaos.

> **Key takeaway:** A shared inbox works when the tool lets everyone see the conversation and the team agrees on three rules: who picks up what, how they show it's taken, and how fast they reply. The rules matter as much as the software.

## The problem with one person owning the phone

When the business phone lives in one person's pocket, a few problems show up again and again:

- **Single point of failure.** If that person is sick, busy or away, customers hit a wall.
- **Invisible history.** Nobody else knows what was promised to whom.
- **Burnout.** The phone owner can never fully switch off.
- **Messy handovers.** When that person leaves, their customer conversations often leave with them, especially if it was their personal number.
- **Slow replies.** One person can only answer one call at a time.

None of this is anyone's fault. It's what happens when a business grows past the point where one phone can carry it.

## How a shared number works

The basic idea is simple: the business number belongs to the business account, not to a person or a SIM card. Team members connect to it through an app on their computer or phone.

In a typical shared setup:

- **Calls** ring several people at once, or in a set order, until someone answers.
- **Texts** land in one inbox that everyone on the team can read.
- **Replies** go out from the business number, no matter who sends them.
- **History** stays with the number, so a new team member can scroll back and see what happened.

| Approach | Who can see texts | Who can answer calls | Tracks who did what |
|---|---|---|---|
| One person's mobile | One person | One person | No |
| Forwarding to several phones | Usually nobody else | One phone at a time | No |
| Shared login to a business app | Everyone signed in | Everyone signed in | No |
| Shared inbox with team seats | Everyone on the team | Everyone on the team | Yes |

The last row is the ideal: each person has their own login, and the system records who answered, who replied and who is handling what.

## Assigning and marking conversations

The most common failure in a shared inbox is the double reply: two people answer the same customer within a minute, often with slightly different information. The fix is a visible signal that a conversation is taken.

Dedicated shared-inbox tools often let you assign a conversation to a person or mark it as open or done. If your tool doesn't, you can get most of the way there with a team agreement:

1. **Claim before you type.** Post a quick message in your team chat: "Taking the Garcia text."
2. **Split by responsibility.** For example, Alex handles booking questions, Sam handles billing.
3. **Split by time.** Morning person covers 8 to 12, afternoon person covers 12 to 5.
4. **Close the loop.** When a conversation is resolved, say so in the team chat.

It's low-tech, but it works, and it builds the habits you'll want anyway when you move to a tool with built-in assignment.

## Internal notes and handoffs

Customers hate repeating themselves. When one team member hands a conversation to another, the context has to go with it.

Good handoff notes are short and specific:

- "Garcia, leaking tap, quoted $120, wants Thursday after 2. Waiting on parts confirmation."
- "Chen invoice: paid by card, needs receipt emailed to new address."

Some tools let you add private notes inside the conversation that the customer never sees. If yours doesn't, keep notes in one shared place, such as a team chat channel per number or a shared document, and always include the customer's name and number so the note can be found later.

## Setting response-time goals

A shared inbox without response goals tends to drift. Everyone assumes someone else will answer.

Set simple, realistic targets and agree on them as a team:

| Channel | During business hours | Outside business hours |
|---|---|---|
| Calls | Answer within 4 rings, or call back within 30 minutes | Voicemail, returned next morning |
| Texts | Reply within 15 to 30 minutes | Reply by 10am next business day |
| Voicemails | Return within 1 hour | Return by 10am next business day |

Write the targets down, review them after a month, and adjust. If you consistently miss them, that's a sign you need more coverage, not more pressure.

## Rolling it out to your team

Changing how the phone works can unsettle customers and staff, so introduce it in stages:

1. **Get a dedicated business number** if you're still using a personal one. Our guide on [why you shouldn't run your business on your personal number](/blog/stop-using-personal-number-for-business) covers how to move customers over.
2. **Decide coverage rules** before anyone starts answering: who covers which hours and which topics.
3. **Train with real examples.** Walk through a few past conversations and agree how each should have been handled.
4. **Start with calls ringing everyone,** then add shared texting once people are comfortable.
5. **Review weekly for the first month.** Look for missed messages, double replies and slow responses.
6. **Update your listings** so the shared number is the one customers find.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a shared team inbox for phone calls and texts?

It's a setup where one business number's calls and texts are visible to several team members, who can all answer from their own devices. Replies go out from the business number, not from personal phones.

### How do you stop two people replying to the same customer?

Make it visible who has taken a conversation, either with an assign feature in your tool or with a quick "taking this one" message in your team chat. Splitting responsibility by topic or by shift also helps.

### Is it safe to share one login between team members?

It can work for a very small, trusted team, but it isn't ideal. You can't see who did what, and you have to change the password when someone leaves. Individual seats are the better long-term option.

### Does DIGIRINGO support multiple users on one number?

Not yet. Multi-user seats and a shared team inbox are coming soon. Today, calls ring every device signed in to the account, and you can forward unanswered calls to any cellphone.

### How many people should share one business number?

There's no fixed rule, but once more than three or four people answer the same line, clear roles and assignment become essential. Many teams split into separate numbers for sales and support at that point.
