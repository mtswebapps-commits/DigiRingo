"How long will it take?" is the first question anyone asks about porting a phone number, and the honest answer is "it depends." A single mobile number with clean paperwork can move in a day. A business with twenty landline numbers on one account might wait several weeks.

The variation isn't random, though. It comes down to the type of number, the carrier you're leaving, how many numbers are involved, and whether your details match the old carrier's records exactly. Once you understand those factors, you can predict your timeline fairly well and plan around it.

This guide gives realistic ranges for each number type, explains the stages a port goes through, lists the usual causes of delay, and shows how to keep your phones working the whole time.

> **Key takeaway:** Plan for a few business days for a simple mobile port and one to three weeks for business landlines, toll-free numbers or multi-number accounts. Accurate paperwork is the single biggest thing you control.

## Typical timelines by number type

These ranges are typical for the US and Canada. Individual ports can be faster or slower.

| Number type | Typical timeline | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Single mobile number | Same day to 2 business days | Highly automated between mobile carriers |
| Single landline or VoIP number | About 3 to 10 business days | More manual checks, varies by carrier |
| Multiple numbers on one account | 1 to 3 weeks | Every number must be validated, sometimes in batches |
| Partial port (some numbers stay behind) | 1 to 3 weeks or more | The old carrier must split the account |
| Toll-free numbers | A few days to 2 weeks | Handled through a separate toll-free registry process |
| International numbers | Highly variable | Depends on each country's rules |

US rules require carriers to complete "simple" ports within one business day once the request is validated. A simple port is roughly one number, no complex services attached, and complete, correct information. In practice, the steps before and after that window, such as validation, back-and-forth on errors, and the gaining provider's own processing, add time. Anything that isn't simple, such as a business account with many lines, isn't covered by the one-day expectation.

## The stages a port request goes through

Knowing the stages helps you understand where your request is and why it might be waiting.

1. **Preparation.** You gather the account name, service address, account number and PIN, and sign a Letter of Authorization. This can take minutes or days depending on how fast you get the details.
2. **Submission.** Your new provider sends the request to the losing carrier.
3. **Validation.** The losing carrier checks every detail against their records. This is where most delays happen.
4. **Confirmation.** If everything matches, the losing carrier confirms a date and time, known as the FOC (Firm Order Commitment).
5. **Activation.** On the FOC date, routing changes and calls start reaching the new provider.
6. **Completion.** The new provider confirms the port is done and finishes any remaining setup, such as texting.

If the request is rejected at validation, it goes back to step 1 or 2 with a fix. Each round trip can add a few business days.

## What causes delays

Most slow ports are slow for one of these reasons:

- **Mismatched details.** A name spelled differently, an old address, or a missing suite number. Carriers match records strictly.
- **Wrong account number or PIN.** Mobile carriers in particular often use a separate transfer PIN that expires after a while.
- **Account locks.** Some accounts have a port-out freeze or extra security that must be removed first.
- **Partial ports.** Moving only some numbers off an account can require the old carrier to restructure it.
- **Numbers with extra services.** Lines tied to things like alarm systems, fax services or hunt groups may need those removed first.
- **Holidays and weekends.** Ports are processed on business days only.
- **Carrier backlogs.** Some carriers simply move slower than others.

## How to avoid downtime during the switch

Downtime during a port is rare when you plan well. The golden rule is simple: **don't cancel your old service until the port is complete.** If you cancel first, the number can be disconnected and may be lost for good.

Other ways to keep things smooth:

- **Start using the new service early.** Most providers give you a temporary number, so you can learn the app and set up routing before your main number arrives.
- **Forward the old number in the meantime.** Your existing carrier can usually forward calls to your new service while you wait. When the port completes, the forwarding becomes irrelevant because the number now lives with the new provider.
- **Avoid busy days for the cut-over.** If you can choose the FOC date, choose a quieter weekday morning.
- **Have someone watch both services** on port day to catch any calls still arriving on the old line.

| Before the port | During the port | After the port |
|---|---|---|
| Keep old service active | Answer on both services | Test inbound and outbound calls |
| Set up the new service fully | Watch for missed calls | Test texting |
| Forward old number if useful | Don't make account changes | Then cancel the old service |

## Can you text before the port completes?

Not from the number being ported, at least not through the new provider. Until the number is fully moved, texts sent to it still go to your old carrier.

There's also a second step for business texting in the US. Text messages from a local business number need 10DLC registration, and that registration has to be in place with the provider sending the messages. Brand and campaign review can take from one to several days on its own. If texting matters to you, start registration with your new provider early so it's approved by the time the number arrives. Our guide to [A2P 10DLC](/blog/what-is-a2p-10dlc) explains what's involved.

## Tracking your port

You shouldn't have to guess where your port stands. A good provider will tell you:

- When the request was submitted.
- Whether it was accepted or rejected, and why.
- The confirmed FOC date and time.
- When the port is complete.

If you haven't heard anything for several business days, ask. A request may be sitting on a rejection that needs a small correction. Speed of response on your side often matters more than anything the carriers do.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the fastest type of number to port?

A single mobile number with correct details. These often complete the same day or the next business day.

### Why is my business landline taking so long?

Business accounts often have multiple numbers, extra services and stricter checks. Any mismatch in details also triggers a rejection and resubmission.

### Will my phone stop working while I wait?

No. Your number keeps working on your old service until the port date. The switch itself usually causes little or no interruption.

### Do weekends count?

No. Port requests are processed on business days, so a request sent on a Friday afternoon usually isn't looked at until Monday.
