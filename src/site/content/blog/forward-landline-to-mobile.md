Plenty of small businesses still have a landline. It's the number on the shop sign, the one in old directories, and the one long-time customers have saved for years. But the people answering it are rarely sitting next to it anymore.

Forwarding the landline to a mobile phone is the quick fix. It takes a minute to set up and means you won't miss calls while you're out. Longer term, many businesses decide the landline itself isn't worth paying for, and move the number somewhere more flexible.

This guide covers both. First, how to forward a landline to a mobile and what it may cost. Then, the limits of that setup, and a step-by-step plan for retiring the landline while keeping the number customers know.

> **Key takeaway:** Forwarding your landline is a fine short-term fix. For the long term, move the number into a mobile-friendly phone app by porting it, and never cancel the landline until the move is complete.

## Forwarding with your landline carrier

Most landline carriers in the US and Canada support call forwarding with star codes dialled from the landline phone itself. The most common codes are:

| Action | Common code | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Turn on forwarding of all calls | *72, then the destination number | Wait for a confirmation tone or for the call to connect |
| Turn off forwarding | *73 | Dial from the same landline |
| Forward when busy or unanswered | Varies by carrier | Often a separate paid feature |
| Remote activation | Varies by carrier | Lets you turn forwarding on from another phone |

A typical sequence:

1. Pick up the landline and listen for a dial tone.
2. Dial *72.
3. Dial the full mobile number, including area code.
4. Wait. On many systems the mobile will ring. Answer it, or wait for a confirmation tone, to confirm forwarding.
5. Hang up. Calls to the landline now ring your mobile.

To check it worked, call the landline from another phone. If it rings your mobile, you're set. To stop forwarding, dial *73 from the landline.

Codes and steps vary between carriers and phone systems. If *72 doesn't work, check your carrier's support pages or ask them to enable call forwarding on the line. Office phone systems (PBXs) often have their own forwarding menus instead.

## Costs your carrier may charge

Forwarding isn't always free. Common charges include:

- **A monthly feature fee** for call forwarding, if it's not included in your plan.
- **Per-minute charges** for forwarded calls. When a call is forwarded, your landline effectively places a new call to your mobile, and you may be billed for it as if you'd dialled it.
- **Long-distance or international rates** if the mobile number is in another area or country.
- **Separate fees for conditional forwarding** (busy or no answer) or remote activation.

Check your plan or ask your carrier before relying on forwarding for weeks at a time. Heavy call volume on per-minute forwarding can add up.

## Limits of landline forwarding

Forwarding solves the immediate problem but leaves several gaps:

- **You're still paying for the landline.** Plus any forwarding fees on top.
- **Only one destination.** Carrier forwarding sends calls to a single number, so it can't ring your mobile and a colleague's at once. See [how to make one number ring several phones](/blog/ring-multiple-phones-one-number).
- **Callbacks show your mobile number.** When you return a call from your mobile, the customer sees your personal number, not the business landline.
- **No texting.** Most landlines can't send or receive texts, and forwarding doesn't change that.
- **Voicemail confusion.** Unanswered forwarded calls often land in your personal mobile voicemail.
- **It depends on the copper line or box.** If the landline service has an outage, forwarding may stop too.

| | Landline with forwarding | Number in a business phone app |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | Landline plus forwarding fees | Usually a few dollars per number |
| Rings multiple devices | No | Yes |
| Call back from business number | No, shows mobile | Yes |
| Texting | Rarely | Yes, on supported numbers |
| Works anywhere with internet | No | Yes |

## Porting the landline number to a mobile app

The long-term fix is **porting**: moving the landline number away from the landline carrier into a business phone app. The number stays the same, but it now rings your app, your laptop and your mobile, and you can call and text from it.

What you need:

- The account holder name and **service address** exactly as the landline carrier has them.
- The **account number** and any PIN.
- A signed authorization form from your new provider.

Business landlines are the type of number most often delayed in porting, usually because of mismatched details or extra services on the line, such as DSL, alarm systems or fax. For the full process, see [number porting explained](/blog/what-is-number-porting).

One important caution: **don't cancel the landline first.** If you cancel before the port completes, the number can be disconnected and may be lost.

## Step-by-step migration plan

Here's a calm, low-risk way to retire a landline:

1. **Get your new business phone app set up** with a new number, so you can learn it and configure routing.
2. **Forward the landline** to the new number with *72. Calls now ring the app on all your devices instead of a single mobile.
3. **Gather landline account details** from a recent bill or a Customer Service Record from the carrier.
4. **Check for extra services** on the line, such as internet, alarm monitoring, fax or a card terminal, and plan how to replace them.
5. **Port the landline number** into the new provider when you're ready.
6. **Test on port day.** Call from mobile and landline phones, and check caller ID on outgoing calls.
7. **Cancel the landline** only once the port is complete and tested.
8. **Return or recycle hardware** like rented phones or modems.

## Keeping the old number on listings

The landline number probably appears in a lot of places. If you keep it through porting, you don't need to change any of them. If you move to a new number instead, update:

- Your Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing and other map listings.
- Your website header, footer and contact page.
- Social media profiles.
- Online directories and review sites.
- Email signatures, invoices and quote templates.
- Printed materials, vehicle signage and shop signs.

Keeping the same number is easiest. If you can't port right away, keep the landline forwarding to your new number until you can.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I forward my landline to my cellphone?

On most US and Canadian landlines, dial *72, then the mobile number, and wait for confirmation. The usual code to turn it off is star 73, though codes vary by carrier.

### Does forwarding a landline cost money?

It can. Some carriers charge a monthly feature fee, per-minute charges for forwarded calls, or long-distance rates. Check your plan.

### Can I turn on landline forwarding when I'm not in the office?

Some carriers offer remote activation from another phone, often for an extra fee. Ask your carrier.

### Can I keep my landline number if I cancel the landline?

Yes, if you port it to a new provider before cancelling. If you cancel first, you may lose the number.
