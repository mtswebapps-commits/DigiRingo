If you spend most of your day at a desk, texting customers from a phone is slow. You type with your thumbs, switch between apps to copy an address or a quote, and lose track of which conversation you already answered. A full keyboard and a big screen make the same work faster and less error-prone.

There are a few ways to get your texts onto a computer. Some mirror your personal phone. Others run entirely in the browser with a dedicated business number. They look similar at first, but they behave very differently once you rely on them every day.

This guide compares the options, explains why the number you text from matters as much as the screen you type on, and shows how to get started.

> **Key takeaway:** Phone-mirroring tools are fine for personal use, but for business texting you want a browser-based app tied to a dedicated business number, so the same conversations show up on your computer and your phone and customers always see one consistent number.

## Why texting from a computer saves time

The benefits are practical, not flashy:

- **Faster typing.** A keyboard is quicker than a phone for anything longer than a sentence.
- **Copy and paste.** Pull addresses, order numbers and prices straight from your other tools.
- **Fewer mistakes.** A larger screen makes it easier to check the message and the recipient before sending.
- **Less context switching.** You stay in the same place where you already handle email, invoices and scheduling.
- **Easier backlog work.** Working through ten customer threads in the morning is much more manageable on a big screen.

For many small businesses, texting has become the main way customers get in touch. Treating it like a desk task rather than a pocket task is a natural step.

## Phone-mirroring options and their limits

Most phone makers and operating systems offer a way to see your phone's text messages on a computer. iPhone users can use Messages on a Mac. Android users have options such as Google Messages for web and Microsoft's Phone Link on Windows. These tools pair the computer with your phone and relay messages back and forth.

They're convenient, but they have limits for business:

- **They use your personal number.** Customers get your private mobile number, which is hard to take back later. We cover the downsides in [why you shouldn't run your business on your personal number](/blog/stop-using-personal-number-for-business).
- **They depend on your phone.** If the phone is off, out of battery or far away, some setups stop syncing.
- **Personal and business messages mix.** Your family group chat sits next to a customer's complaint.
- **No business texting registration.** In the US, business messages are expected to come from registered numbers. Your personal line isn't set up for that, and high-volume or marketing-style texts from it may be filtered.
- **Hard to hand over.** If you hire someone or step away, there's no clean way to give them the customer conversations without giving them your phone.

Mirroring is a good personal convenience. It's a weak foundation for a business.

## Browser-based business texting

The alternative is a business phone app that runs in a web browser. You sign in at a website, and your business number's text conversations are right there. There's nothing to pair, and your phone doesn't need to be nearby.

What to look for:

- **A dedicated business number** that you own on the account, separate from your mobile line.
- **Threaded conversations per number,** so every customer has one tidy history.
- **Delivery receipts,** so you know a message actually reached the carrier and the handset.
- **Business texting registration support,** such as a guided A2P 10DLC process for US numbers.
- **The same inbox on mobile,** so you can reply from your phone when you're away from the desk.
- **Calling too,** so customers who prefer to call reach the same number.

The trade-off is cost: you pay for the number and for messages. For most small businesses that's a few dollars a month, which is usually less than the time saved.

## Keeping the same number on desktop and mobile

The biggest win of a business app is consistency. Whether you reply from a laptop at the office or your phone in a parking lot, the customer sees the same number and the same thread continues.

That matters because:

- Customers save one contact for your business and it always works.
- You don't have to remember which device a conversation started on.
- Calls and texts stay connected to the same identity.

When you choose an app, check that it offers a mobile app or a mobile-friendly web version as well as the desktop experience, and that messages sync between them without delay.

## Sending pictures and files

Pictures are useful in business texting: a photo of a part, a screenshot of an error, a scan of a signed form. That requires MMS (picture messaging), which is a separate capability from plain SMS. Not every business texting app supports it, and support varies by number type and country.

If your app doesn't support MMS, a common workaround is to upload the file somewhere you control, like your website or a cloud folder, and text a link using your own domain. Avoid public link shorteners, since carriers often filter messages that contain them. Our guide to [SMS vs MMS](/blog/sms-vs-mms-business) explains the differences and costs in more detail.

## A simple desk texting routine

Tools help, but habits do most of the work:

1. **Check the inbox at set times,** for example on arrival, after lunch and before closing.
2. **Answer the oldest thread first** so nobody waits twice as long as everyone else.
3. **Keep saved replies** in a document for common questions like hours, directions and pricing.
4. **Include your business name** in the first message of a new thread.
5. **Respect opt-outs.** If someone replies STOP, don't message them again.
6. **Move complex issues to a call.** If a thread goes past five or six messages, a two-minute call is usually faster.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I text from my computer without my phone turned on?

With a browser-based business texting app, yes. The number lives with the provider, not on your phone, so the computer works on its own. Phone-mirroring tools usually need your phone nearby and connected.

### Will customers see a different number if I text from my computer?

Not if you use a business app with one number on all devices. Customers see the same business number whether you reply from the browser or the mobile app.

### Is it legal to text customers from a computer?

The device doesn't matter. What matters is that customers have agreed to hear from you, you honor opt-outs, and in the US your number is registered for business texting.

### Can I send photos from the DIGIRINGO web app?
