Most people don't think about the difference between a text and a picture message. On a personal phone, you just hit send. For a business, though, the difference shows up in cost, delivery and what you can actually send.

SMS is the plain text message: short, cheap and supported by every mobile phone. MMS adds pictures, video, audio or longer content, at a higher price and with more ways for things to go wrong. Both have a place in business messaging, and knowing when to use each saves money and keeps your messages arriving.

This guide covers how each works, why long messages get split into segments, how pricing is calculated, when a picture is worth the extra cost, file size limits, and how group texts fit in.

> **Key takeaway:** Use SMS for almost everything: reminders, confirmations, replies and links. Use MMS when a picture genuinely does the job better than words, like showing a finished job or a product. Keep SMS within one segment where you can, and keep MMS files small.

## The quick difference

| | SMS | MMS |
|---|---|---|
| Stands for | Short Message Service | Multimedia Messaging Service |
| Content | Text only | Images, GIFs, short video, audio, longer text |
| Length | 160 characters per segment (fewer with emoji) | Much longer text plus media |
| Cost | Lowest | Usually several times the price of an SMS |
| Delivery | Very reliable on every phone | Depends on the recipient's phone and data settings |
| Speed | Near instant | Can be slower, especially with large files |
| Best for | Reminders, confirmations, conversations | Photos, product images, visual instructions |

A link inside an SMS is often a good middle ground. Instead of sending a photo by MMS, you can send a short text with a link to the image on your website.

## Character limits and message segments

SMS was designed with a hard limit per message. Longer messages still work, but they're split into parts called segments behind the scenes and stitched back together on the recipient's phone.

The limit depends on the characters you use:

- **Standard characters (GSM-7 encoding):** Basic English letters, numbers and common punctuation. One segment holds up to **160 characters**. When a message runs longer, each segment holds **153 characters**, because a few characters are used to stitch the parts together.
- **Unicode characters (UCS-2 encoding):** Emoji, many non-Latin alphabets, and some special symbols. One segment holds up to **70 characters**, or **67 per segment** in longer messages.

| Encoding | Single message | Per segment in long messages | Triggered by |
|---|---|---|---|
| GSM-7 | 160 characters | 153 characters | Plain English text |
| UCS-2 | 70 characters | 67 characters | Any emoji or many special characters |

The catch: a single emoji switches the whole message to the shorter limit. A 150-character message that fits in one segment becomes three segments if you add a smiley face. Curly "smart" quotes pasted from a word processor can sometimes do the same thing.

## How pricing works per segment

Many providers and carriers count SMS usage by segment rather than by message. That means a long message, or one with an emoji, can cost two or three times as much as a short one.

Here's how a few common messages break down:

| Message | Characters | Encoding | Segments |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Reminder: your appointment is tomorrow at 10am. Reply C to confirm." | About 70 | GSM-7 | 1 |
| A 300-character service update in plain text | 300 | GSM-7 | 2 |
| A 140-character message with one emoji | 140 | UCS-2 | 2 |
| A 480-character message in plain text | 480 | GSM-7 | 4 |

MMS is priced differently: usually a flat rate per message that's higher than an SMS, regardless of text length, though very large files may be compressed or rejected. Carrier pass-through fees, where they apply, are also typically higher for MMS than SMS.

Ways to keep costs down:

- Keep routine texts under 160 characters.
- Skip emoji in high-volume messages.
- Replace a long explanation with a short text and a link.
- Check how your provider counts segments before you send in bulk.

## Best uses for MMS in business

A picture is worth paying for when it replaces a phone call or a long explanation.

- **Before-and-after photos** for cleaning, landscaping, auto detailing or repairs.
- **Proof of work** like a photo of a delivered package or a completed installation.
- **Product images** for a shop showing a customer the item they asked about.
- **Visual instructions** such as where to park, which door to use, or how to reset a device.
- **Event flyers or menus** for restaurants and venues, sent to customers who opted in.
- **Receiving photos from customers,** like a picture of a broken appliance before a service visit.

Where MMS usually isn't worth it: appointment reminders, payment reminders, confirmations and short replies. Plain SMS does those just as well for less.

## File types and size limits

MMS limits are set by carriers and vary, so the safest approach is to aim small.

| File type | Common formats | Practical guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Images | JPG, PNG, GIF | Keep under about 500 KB to 1 MB for reliable delivery |
| Animated GIFs | GIF | Short and small; large GIFs are often compressed or fail |
| Video | MP4, 3GP | Very short clips only; many carriers cap total size tightly |
| Audio | MP3, AMR | Short clips |
| Documents | PDF and others | Support is inconsistent; a link is usually more reliable |

Carriers may compress large images, which can make them look blurry. Some carriers accept larger files than others, but if you're sending to customers on many networks, the lowest common limit is the one that counts. For anything large, host the file and send a link.

## Group texting considerations

Group texts (one message thread with several recipients who can all see each other's replies) use MMS behind the scenes on most phones, even if the content is just text.

Things to consider before using group texts for business:

- **Privacy.** Everyone in the group sees everyone else's number. Never group customers who don't know each other.
- **Reply chaos.** Replies go to the whole group, which can get noisy.
- **Cost.** Since group texts generally travel as MMS, they cost more per message.
- **Compliance.** Each recipient still needs to have agreed to receive your messages.

For sending the same update to many customers, individual messages are usually better: private, cheaper as SMS, and easier to manage replies.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many characters can a business SMS have?

A single SMS holds up to 160 standard characters. Longer messages are split into segments of 153 characters each, and messages with emoji drop to 70 characters, or 67 per segment.

### Do emojis make texts more expensive?

They can. One emoji switches the message to a shorter character limit, so a message that fit in one segment may be split into two or more, and many providers count usage by segment.

### Is MMS delivery less reliable than SMS?

Usually a little. MMS depends on the recipient's phone settings, mobile data and carrier file limits, so large files can be compressed, delayed or rejected. Plain SMS works on virtually every phone.

### Can I send a picture without MMS?

Yes. Upload the image to your website or a file-sharing service and send the link in a regular SMS. Use your own domain rather than a public link shortener for better delivery.
