The hardest part of texting customers is often the first few words. Too formal and it sounds like a robot. Too casual and it sounds like spam. Too long and it gets split into several messages and costs more.

Below are 50 templates you can copy, adjust and send. They're grouped by situation and written to be short (most fit in a single 160-character segment), to name the business up front, and to be clear about what the customer should do next. Promotional templates include opt-out wording.

Swap the details in square brackets for your own. Before you send any of these, make sure the customer has agreed to receive texts from you; our [SMS opt-in and consent guide](/blog/sms-opt-in-consent-guide) explains what that means.

> **Key takeaway:** A good business text names your business, says one thing, tells the customer what to do next, and stays under 160 characters. Marketing messages also need clear consent and "Reply STOP to opt out."

## Rules for writing a good business text

| Rule | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Name your business first | Recipients and carriers need to know who's texting | "Greenleaf Landscaping: ..." |
| One message, one purpose | Easier to read and act on | A reminder is just a reminder |
| Give a clear next step | Gets replies | "Reply C to confirm" |
| Stay under 160 characters | Avoids extra segments and cost | Cut filler words |
| Skip emoji in routine texts | Emoji shorten the segment limit | Use plain punctuation |
| Avoid public link shorteners | They trigger carrier filters | Use your own domain |
| Add opt-out to promotions | Required by good practice and carriers | "Reply STOP to opt out" |
| Text at reasonable hours | Respect the customer's time zone | 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. local time |

For why emoji and length affect cost, see [SMS vs MMS for business](/blog/sms-vs-mms-business).

## Appointment reminders and confirmations

1. "[Business]: Hi [Name], your appointment is booked for [Day, Date] at [Time]. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule."
2. "[Business]: Reminder, you're booked tomorrow at [Time]. Reply C to confirm. Need to change it? Just reply here."
3. "[Business]: See you today at [Time], [Name]. We're at [Address]. Reply if you're running late."
4. "[Business]: Thanks, [Name]. Your appointment on [Date] at [Time] is confirmed."
5. "[Business]: Your appointment on [Date] has been rescheduled to [New Date] at [Time]. Reply if that doesn't work."
6. "[Business]: Your appointment on [Date] is cancelled. Reply here or call [Phone] to book a new time."
7. "[Business]: Hi [Name], our technician [Tech] is on the way and should arrive in about [X] minutes."
8. "[Business]: We're running about [X] minutes behind today. Sorry for the wait, [Name]. Reply if you'd prefer to reschedule."
9. "[Business]: We have an opening tomorrow at [Time]. Want it? Reply YES and it's yours."
10. "[Business]: Hi [Name], it's been [6 months] since your last visit. Reply BOOK and we'll find you a time."

## Payment and invoice reminders

19. "[Business]: Hi [Name], invoice #[Number] for [amount] is ready. Pay online at [yourdomain.com/pay]."
20. "[Business]: Friendly reminder that invoice #[Number] for [amount] is due on [Date]."
21. "[Business]: Invoice #[Number] is due today. If you've already paid, thank you and please ignore this."
22. "[Business]: Invoice #[Number] for [amount] is now [X] days past due. Reply or call [Phone] if you need to talk about it."
23. "[Business]: Thanks, [Name]! We received your payment of [amount]."
24. "[Business]: Your card payment for [service] didn't go through. Please update it at [yourdomain.com/billing]."
25. "[Business]: Your [plan/service] renews on [Date] for [amount]. Reply with any questions."
26. "[Business]: Your deposit of [amount] secures your booking on [Date]. Pay at [yourdomain.com/deposit]."

Never ask for card numbers, passwords or security codes by text. Send a link to a secure page on your own site instead.

## Review and feedback requests

35. "[Business]: Thanks for coming in, [Name]! How did we do? Reply with a number from 1 to 5."
36. "[Business]: Glad we could help, [Name]. Would you leave us a quick review? [yourdomain.com/review]"
37. "[Business]: Your feedback helps us improve. Got 30 seconds? [yourdomain.com/survey]"
38. "[Business]: Sorry to hear that, [Name]. Our manager [Name] will call you today to make it right."
39. "[Business]: Thank you for the kind review, [Name]! We really appreciate it."
40. "[Business]: Know someone who needs [service]? Share our number. Thanks for the support, [Name]."

Ask for reviews only from customers who are happy to receive texts from you, and don't offer rewards in exchange for positive reviews; many review sites forbid it.

44. "[Business]: Thanks for being a regular, [Name]. Show this text for [offer] until [Date]. Reply STOP to opt out."

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a business text message be?

Aim for under 160 characters so the message fits in a single segment. If you need to say more, send a link to a page on your website instead of a long text.

### Should business texts include the company name?

Yes. Starting with your business name tells the customer who's texting and helps your messages look legitimate to carrier filters.

### Do all business texts need "Reply STOP to opt out"?

Promotional texts should include it. For conversational replies and service messages, including opt-out instructions in the first message and periodically after is a common good practice.

### Can I personalize templates with the customer's name?

Yes, and it usually helps. Personalized messages feel less like bulk marketing to customers and filters alike. Double-check names before sending.

### When is the best time to send business texts?

Generally between 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. in the customer's local time, on weekdays where possible. Avoid early mornings and late evenings, and check any state rules for marketing messages.
