When you start selling to customers in another country, one of the first things they notice is your phone number. A foreign number makes people hesitate. They wonder if the call will be expensive, if anyone will understand them, and whether you're really able to serve them.

A local virtual number in that country removes most of that friction. Customers dial a number that looks familiar, and the call reaches you wherever you are. You don't need an office, a local SIM card or a local employee to get one.

What surprises many businesses is the paperwork. Unlike US numbers, which you can usually activate in minutes, many countries require proof of who you are and sometimes a local address before a number goes live. This guide explains why, how long it takes, what the numbers can and can't do, and how to apply.

> **Key takeaway:** A local number abroad builds trust fast, but each country has its own rules. Expect to provide ID or an address in many countries, allow a few days for review, and check whether texting is supported before you rely on it.

## Why a local number beats asking customers to call abroad

Asking a customer in London or Sydney to dial a US number puts the work on them. Even when their plan includes international calling, the unfamiliar format makes them pause. Many simply send an email instead, or go with a competitor that looks local.

A local number helps in a few clear ways:

- **No surprise costs for the caller.** A call to a local number is billed like any other local or national call on their plan.
- **More answered outbound calls.** People are more likely to pick up a number from their own country than one with a foreign country code.
- **A local presence without an office.** You can list the number on a localized website, in directories and in ads for that market.
- **Easy testing of new markets.** A number costs a few dollars a month, so you can try a market before investing in staff or premises there.

## Countries that need documents and why

Telecom regulators in many countries want to know who holds each phone number. The goals are fraud prevention, emergency services, and stopping numbers from being used for scams. As a result, providers must collect and verify information before activating a number.

Requirements vary widely, and they change over time, so always check the current rules for the specific country. In general they fall into a few levels:

| Requirement level | What's usually asked | Examples of where it's common |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal | Name and contact details | Some countries with lighter regulation |
| Identity | Government ID or business registration | Many European countries |
| Local address | Proof of an address in the country or region | Several EU countries, some area-specific numbers |
| Strict | Local business entity and detailed documents | A smaller number of tightly regulated markets |

Some patterns worth knowing:

- **Geographic numbers often need a local address.** A number tied to a specific city may require an address in that city or region.
- **National or mobile-style numbers can have different rules** from city numbers in the same country.
- **Business vs personal use matters.** Some countries ask for a company registration document if the number is for a business.
- **Documents must match.** The name on your ID or registration should match the name on the account. Mismatches are one of the most common reasons for delays.

## Typical approval timelines

Once you've submitted documents, the carrier reviews them. In many cases this takes one to three business days. It can be faster for countries with light requirements and slower if something needs to be corrected or resubmitted.

To keep things moving:

1. **Have documents ready before you order.** A clear scan or photo of your ID, business registration and proof of address.
2. **Use the same name everywhere.** Account name, document name and business name should line up.
3. **Respond quickly to requests.** If the carrier asks for something else, answer the same day.
4. **Don't leave an order waiting.** Orders that sit without the required documents may be cancelled by the carrier after a short window, and you'd have to start again.

Planning a launch? Order the number a week or two ahead so it's live before your website, ads or printed materials go out.

## Calling and texting capabilities differ by country

Not every virtual number can do everything. Before you choose one, check what it supports.

- **Inbound calls.** Almost all virtual numbers can receive calls.
- **Outbound caller ID.** Most can be used as caller ID when you call out, but some countries have restrictions on displaying certain number types.
- **SMS.** Texting support varies a lot. Many geographic (city) numbers in Europe can't send or receive SMS at all, while mobile-range numbers often can. Some countries also require extra registration for business texting.
- **Toll-free numbers abroad.** These exist in many countries but may only be reachable from inside that country, and some mobile networks charge callers for them.

| Capability | Usually available | Check carefully |
|---|---|---|
| Receive calls | Yes, almost everywhere | Rarely an issue |
| Call out with local caller ID | Most countries | Some number types are restricted |
| Send and receive SMS | Some countries and number types | Often not on city numbers |
| Toll-free reachable from mobiles | Varies | Callers may be charged |

If texting is central to your plan for a country, confirm SMS support for the exact number type before you commit.

## Costs to expect

International numbers cost more than US numbers, and prices vary by country and number type. You'll usually see:

- **A monthly number fee.** Often a few dollars a month for common countries, more for mobile-range or toll-free numbers and for countries with high regulatory costs.
- **Per-minute call rates.** Calls to and from international numbers are charged per minute, and calls to mobile phones abroad usually cost more than calls to landlines.
- **SMS rates** where texting is supported, priced per message by destination country.
- **Possible setup fees** for some countries or number types.

## How to apply step by step

The process is similar with most providers:

1. **Choose the country and number type.** City number, national number, mobile-range or toll-free.
2. **Check the requirements** shown for that country before ordering.
3. **Prepare your documents.** ID, business registration and proof of address as required.
4. **Place the order and upload documents.**
5. **Wait for carrier review**, usually a few business days.
6. **Test the number** once it's live: call it from a local phone if you can, call out with it as caller ID, and send a test text if SMS is supported.
7. **Publish it** on your localized website, profiles and marketing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to live in a country to get a number there?

Usually not, but some countries or number types require a local address. Requirements are shown before you order.

### How long does it take to activate an international number?

Countries with light rules can be fast. Where documents are required, carrier review usually takes 1 to 3 business days.

### Can I text from an international number?

It depends on the country and number type. Many city numbers in Europe don't support SMS, while some mobile-range numbers do. Check before you buy.

### Will customers pay extra to call my number?

No. A local number is billed to the caller like any local or national call on their plan. Some toll-free numbers abroad may cost mobile callers money, though.

### Can I keep the number if I stop using the market?

As long as you keep paying the monthly fee, the number stays yours. If you cancel it, it goes back to the carrier and may be given to someone else.
