When a potential customer calls and nobody answers, they rarely wait. Many don't leave a voicemail. They hang up and call the next business on the list. For a small business that can't answer every call, that's a steady leak of work.

Missed-call text back is a simple fix. When a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text from the same number within seconds: "Sorry we missed you, how can we help?" Instead of a dead end, the caller now has an easy way to keep talking to you, on their own time.

This guide covers why the approach works, how automatic text-back is set up on systems that offer it, templates that get replies, sensible timing and business-hours rules, compliance points, and how to do the same thing manually when automation isn't available.

> **Key takeaway:** The value of text back is speed. A reply within minutes of a missed call catches the caller while they still need you. Whether it's automatic or sent by hand, keep it short, name your business, and ask one simple question.

## What a missed call costs a small business

You don't need industry statistics to see the cost. Look at your own numbers.

1. Count your missed calls in a typical week from your call history.
2. Estimate how many of those were new customers rather than existing ones or spam.
3. Estimate what share of new callers usually become paying customers.
4. Multiply by your average job or order value.

| Example input | Value |

## How auto text-back works

On phone systems that offer it, missed-call text back is a rule tied to a phone number:

1. A call comes in to your business number.
2. Nobody answers within the ring time, or the caller hangs up before voicemail.
3. The system detects a missed call from a mobile number.
4. It sends a pre-written text from the same business number to the caller.
5. If the caller replies, the reply appears in your normal text conversation with them, and you take it from there.

Good implementations include a few safeguards:

- **Only text mobile numbers.** Landlines can't receive texts.
- **Don't text the same caller repeatedly.** If someone calls three times in ten minutes, they should get one text, not three.
- **Skip blocked or opted-out numbers.** Anyone who replied STOP shouldn't get texts.
- **Use different messages during and after business hours.**

Because the text comes from your business number, the caller recognizes it and can reply directly, or call back the same number.

## Templates that get replies

The best text-back messages feel like a person wrote them. Keep them under 160 characters and end with an easy question.

### General

- "Hi, it's [Business]. Sorry we missed your call! How can we help? Reply here and we'll get right back to you."
- "[Business] here. We couldn't pick up just now. What can we help you with today?"

### Service businesses

- "Hi, this is [Business]. We're on a job right now. What do you need help with, and what's your address?"
- "[Business]: sorry we missed you. Is this about a new job or an existing booking? Reply and we'll text you back shortly."

### Appointments and bookings

- "Hi from [Business]! Missed your call. Want to book a time? Reply with a day that works and we'll find a slot."
- "[Business]: sorry we missed you. Reply BOOK to schedule, or tell us what you need."

### After hours

- "[Business]: we're closed right now and open again at [Time] tomorrow. Reply with what you need and we'll get back to you first thing."
- "[Business]: thanks for calling. We're closed, but if this is urgent, reply URGENT and we'll do our best to help tonight."

## Timing and business-hours rules

Timing is what makes text back work, but it needs some limits.

| Situation | Suggested behavior |
|---|---|
| During business hours | Send within a minute or two of the missed call; follow up personally soon after any reply |
| After hours, evening | Send a closed-hours message that says when you'll reply |
| Late night or early morning | Consider waiting until morning, especially for non-urgent businesses |
| Repeat calls from same number | One text per caller within a set window, such as a few hours |
| Existing conversation in progress | Skip the auto text; reply personally instead |

Whatever the setup, the text only helps if someone actually answers the replies. Decide who watches the conversation thread and how quickly they should respond. A text-back that leads to another hour of silence just moves the frustration.

## Compliance notes

Missed-call text back is generally treated as a conversational, customer-initiated message: the person called you first, and you're responding to that call. Even so, a few rules apply. This is general information, not legal advice.

- **Register your number.** In the US, texts from a local number need A2P 10DLC registration. Describe missed-call text back in your campaign so reviewers understand it.
- **Keep it a response, not a pitch.** The first message should be about helping with their call. Don't add promotions or coupons, which moves it toward marketing and needs stronger consent.
- **Don't add callers to marketing lists.** A call is not consent to receive promotional texts.
- **Honor STOP.** If someone replies STOP, don't text them again.
- **Respect quiet hours.** Be careful about texting late at night in the caller's time zone.

## Doing it manually: a simple routine

If your phone system doesn't offer automatic text back, a manual routine gets you most of the benefit.

1. **Check missed calls at set times,** such as every hour, or between every job.
2. **Text first, then call.** A text is quicker for both sides and the customer can reply when they're free.
3. **Keep a few messages saved** in a notes app so you can paste and personalize them in seconds.
4. **Mark them done.** Once you've replied, note it so nobody on the team texts the same caller twice.
5. **Review weekly.** Look at how many missed calls got a reply and how quickly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is missed-call text back legal?

Generally yes, when it's a direct response to someone who just called you and the message is about helping with their call. Register your number for business texting, keep promotions out of it, and honor STOP replies.

### What should a missed-call text say?

Name your business, apologize briefly for missing the call, and ask one simple question like "How can we help?" Keep it under 160 characters so it reads like a personal message.

### Will the text come from my business number?

It should. Texting from the same number the customer called means they recognize it and can reply or call back directly. On DIGIRINGO, manual replies are sent from the business number you choose.

### What if the caller was on a landline?

Landlines can't receive texts, so the message won't arrive. Good automatic systems skip landlines; if you're texting manually, call back instead when a text fails.
