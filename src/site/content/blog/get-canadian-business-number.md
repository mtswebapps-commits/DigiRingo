Canada and the US share the same phone numbering system, so a Canadian number looks exactly like an American one: a three-digit area code and a seven-digit number. To a caller in Toronto or Vancouver, though, the area code says a lot. A familiar local code tells them you're part of their market. A US code, or an international number, tells them you're somewhere else.

That's why businesses expanding into Canada, Canadian startups that don't want a traditional phone line, and remote teams serving Canadian clients often add a Canadian number. With a virtual phone service, you can have one in minutes and answer it from anywhere.

This guide covers who benefits, how to pick an area code by province, local vs toll-free in Canada, how texting rules differ from the US, and how to get set up.

> **Key takeaway:** A Canadian business number gives you a local presence in a province without an office or phone line there. Pick an area code your customers recognize, use a local number for calls and texting, consider a toll-free number for national reach, and follow Canada's consent rules for commercial texts.

## Who needs a Canadian number

A Canadian number is worth having if you:

- **Are a US business selling into Canada** and want Canadian customers to see a familiar number.
- **Are a Canadian business** that wants a business line separate from your personal mobile, without installing a landline.
- **Work remotely or abroad** but serve Canadian clients, for example as a consultant, agency or freelancer.
- **Are opening in a new province** and want a local presence before you have a physical location.
- **Run ads or listings in Canada** and want a number that matches the market.

Because Canada and the US are both in the North American Numbering Plan, calls between the two are dialed like domestic long-distance calls. Even so, many Canadians still prefer calling a local number, and a local number usually gets better answer rates when you call out.

## Picking a province and area code

Canada's larger cities have several area codes, because new codes are added as overlays when numbers run out. All codes in an overlay area cover the same region, so a newer code works just as well as the original. Older codes are often more recognizable but harder to find.

How to choose:

1. **Pick the city where most of your customers are.** If they're mostly in the Greater Toronto Area, a Toronto code is the obvious choice.
2. **If you serve several provinces,** you can hold a local number in each, or use a single toll-free number for national reach.
3. **If you're not sure,** a number in a major business hub like Toronto, Montreal or Vancouver is widely recognized.

## Local vs toll-free in Canada

Toll-free numbers in North America (800, 888, 877, 866, 855, 844, 833) generally work across both Canada and the US. That makes a toll-free number a convenient choice for a business serving customers in both countries, or across all of Canada.

| | Canadian local number | North American toll-free number |
|---|---|---|
| Looks like | (416) 555-0123 | (888) 555-0123 |
| Signals | Local, part of the community | National, established |
| Who pays for incoming calls | Caller's usual rates | The business |
| Reach | Feels local in one region | Works across Canada and the US |

Many businesses use both: a toll-free number on the website and local numbers for outbound calls and conversations with customers. For a deeper look at the trade-offs, see [local or toll-free: which should your business choose](/blog/local-vs-toll-free-number).

## Texting rules in Canada vs the US

Texting is where Canada and the US differ most, and it's worth understanding before you send your first message.

**In the US**, carriers require business texting from local numbers to be registered under A2P 10DLC, which involves registering your brand and campaign. The [A2P 10DLC explainer](/blog/what-is-a2p-10dlc) covers it in plain language.

**In Canada**, the rules come from two directions:

- **Carrier requirements.** Canadian carriers have their own policies for business messaging, and many providers apply a registration process to Canadian numbers too, especially for messages that cross into the US. Expect your provider to ask for business and use-case details before enabling texting.
- **Anti-spam law.** Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) applies to commercial electronic messages, including text messages. In general, you need the recipient's consent before sending commercial messages, you must identify your business, and you must include a working way to unsubscribe. CASL distinguishes between express consent and certain kinds of implied consent, such as an existing business relationship, which can expire. If you send marketing texts at scale, it's worth getting proper advice.

Practical tips for Canadian texting:

- Get clear consent and keep a record of it.
- Identify your business in the first message.
- Honor STOP and other opt-out replies promptly.
- Keep messages relevant and avoid sending at odd hours.

## Setting up greetings and hours

Customers in Canada expect a business line to sound like one. A few things to plan for:

**Greetings.** A short, friendly greeting that names your business works best. If you serve Quebec or a French-speaking market, a bilingual approach is good practice and often expected, and Quebec has its own rules on the use of French in business. For example: "Thanks for calling Northern Supply. Merci d'avoir appelé Northern Supply. Please leave a message, and we'll call you back."

**Business hours.** Canada spans six time zones, from Newfoundland to the Pacific. If you're answering from another province or another country, make your hours clear on your website and in your listings, including the time zone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a Canadian phone number if I'm not in Canada?

Yes. A virtual Canadian number works over the internet, so you can use it from the US or anywhere else with a connection.

### Are calls between Canada and the US expensive?

Not with VoIP. On DIGIRINGO, calls within the US and Canada cost $0.015 per minute, whichever of the two countries you're calling.

### Which Canadian area code should I choose?

Pick the code for the city where most of your customers are, such as 416 or 647 for Toronto, 514 for Montreal, or 604 for Vancouver. Overlay codes in the same area work the same way.

### Do I need consent to text customers in Canada?

Generally, yes. Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation requires consent for commercial text messages, clear identification of your business, and an easy way to unsubscribe.

### Do toll-free numbers work in Canada?

North American toll-free numbers can generally be dialed from both Canada and the US, which makes them a good fit for businesses serving both countries.
