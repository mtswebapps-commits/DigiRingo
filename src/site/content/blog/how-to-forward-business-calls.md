Call forwarding is one of the oldest phone features there is, and still one of the most useful. It sends calls from one number to another, so a call to your office line can ring your cellphone, or a call to your business number can reach a colleague while you're on holiday.

The trouble is that there are several ways to do it, and they behave differently. Forwarding set on your phone works one way, carrier codes work another, and a business phone app works another again. Picking the wrong one can mean callers hitting your personal voicemail, or customers getting your personal number when you call them back.

This guide shows every main option, step by step, and explains which one fits which situation.

> **Key takeaway:** Phone and carrier forwarding are fine for a quick fix. For a business line, forwarding inside a business phone app is better, because you can answer in the app first, fall back to your cellphone, and still call customers back from your business number.

## Forwarding options at a glance

| Method | Where you set it | Best for | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone settings | Phone settings menu | Temporary forwarding of your mobile number | Not available on every carrier, all-or-nothing |
| Android settings | Phone app settings | Same as above, with conditional options | Menus vary by manufacturer |
| Carrier star codes | Dialled from the phone | Landlines and phones without a settings menu | Codes differ between carriers |
| Business phone app | App or web dashboard | Business numbers, multiple devices | Requires using the app |

Before you start, know the difference between **unconditional** forwarding (every call goes to the other number and your phone never rings) and **conditional** forwarding (calls go to the other number only when you're busy, don't answer or are unreachable). We cover conditional rules in depth in [conditional call forwarding](/blog/conditional-call-forwarding).

## Forwarding on iPhone

iPhones have a built-in switch for unconditional forwarding.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Phone**. On recent iOS versions, Phone is listed under **Apps**.
3. Tap **Call Forwarding**.
4. Turn on **Call Forwarding** and enter the number to forward to.
5. Go back. Forwarding is now active.

A few things to know:

- The iPhone menu only does **unconditional** forwarding. For "forward when I don't answer," use carrier codes instead.
- On some carriers the menu doesn't appear at all. Use the carrier codes below.
- Forwarded calls may use minutes on your plan, and forwarding to an international number can be charged by your carrier.

## Forwarding on Android

Android phones usually offer both unconditional and conditional options, but the menu path depends on the manufacturer.

A typical path:

1. Open the **Phone** app.
2. Tap the **three dots** menu, then **Settings**.
3. Look for **Calls**, **Calling accounts**, **Supplementary services** or **More settings**.
4. Tap **Call forwarding**, then choose voice calls if asked.
5. Pick a condition: **Always forward**, **When busy**, **When unanswered** or **When unreachable**.
6. Enter the number and tap **Enable** or **Turn on**.

If you have two SIMs, you'll usually choose which SIM to set forwarding on first. If you can't find the menu, the carrier codes below work on most Android phones too.

## Carrier star codes

Star codes are short codes you dial like a phone number. They're handled by the carrier, so they work even on basic phones and many landlines. Codes vary, so always confirm with your carrier. The table uses "N" to stand for the number you're forwarding to.

| What it does | GSM-style mobile networks | Common US landline and some mobile codes |
|---|---|---|
| Forward all calls | Dial **21, star, N, # | Dial *72, then N |
| Cancel forwarding all calls | Dial ##21# | Dial *73 |
| Forward when not answered | Dial **61, star, N, # | Varies by carrier |
| Forward when busy | Dial **67, star, N, # | Varies by carrier |
| Forward when unreachable | Dial **62, star, N, # | Varies by carrier |
| Cancel all forwarding | Dial ##002# | Varies by carrier |

For example, on a GSM-style network, forwarding all calls to 555-0100 in area code 212 means dialling two stars, 21, a star, 2125550100 and a hash.

Tips for using star codes:

- Include the full number with area code, and the country code if your carrier needs it.
- Listen for a confirmation tone or message. Some carriers ring the destination briefly to confirm.
- Business landlines may need forwarding activated on the account before codes work.
- Some carriers charge for forwarded minutes, especially on landlines. Check your plan.

If you're specifically moving away from an office line, see [how to forward a landline to a mobile phone](/blog/forward-landline-to-mobile).

## Forwarding from a business number app

Phone and carrier forwarding move calls from one number to another. A business phone app works differently: your business number lives in the app, and the app decides where calls go.

That gives you a few advantages:

- **Ring the app first, then your cellphone.** You can answer on your laptop or in the app, and only fall back to your personal phone if you're away.
- **Less chance of personal voicemail grabbing business calls.** The app controls what happens when nobody answers in the app.
- **Call back from your business number.** When you return a call from the app, customers see your business number, not your personal one.
- **Separate settings per number.** A sales line and a support line can each route differently.
- **No carrier codes or menus.** Settings live in one place and are easy to change.

## Common forwarding problems

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Calls go to voicemail instead of forwarding | Your phone's voicemail answers before forwarding kicks in | Shorten the no-answer delay or use an app with its own timer |
| Forwarding code not accepted | Wrong code for your carrier | Check your carrier's support page |
| Forwarded calls don't show the caller's number | Carrier or device behaviour | Ask your carrier; it varies |
| Forwarding fails while abroad | Roaming rules | Forwarding while roaming can be charged or blocked |
| Customers see your personal number on callbacks | You called back from your cell | Call back from the business app instead |
| You forgot forwarding was on | Unconditional forwarding left active | Cancel it with your carrier's cancel code or in settings |

## Frequently asked questions

### Does call forwarding cost extra?

It depends. Many mobile plans include forwarding, but some carriers charge for forwarded minutes, especially on landlines and to international destinations. With a business app, forwarded calls are billed at the app's per-minute rate.

### Can I forward calls to an international number?

Usually yes, but carriers may charge international rates. Check your plan, or use an app with published per-country rates.

### Why do forwarded calls still go to my voicemail?

Your phone's voicemail may answer before forwarding kicks in, or forwarding may only be set for some conditions. Check which conditions are active.

### Will the person I forward to see who's calling?

Often they'll see the original caller's number, but it varies by carrier and setup.

### How do I turn forwarding off?

Switch it off in your phone's settings, dial your carrier's cancel code, or change the route in your business phone app.
